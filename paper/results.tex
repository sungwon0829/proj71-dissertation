\subsection{Judge agreement}
\label{sec:results:kappa}

Two agreement figures are reported side by side and neither replaces the other.

\paragraph{Inter-model agreement (measured).} On held-out sets disjoint from any set used
to tune the corresponding prompt, and against reference labels produced by a language-model
agent rather than a person: attack-success judge $\kappa = 0.521$, 95\% CI $[0.29, 0.76]$,
recall 0.55, precision 1.00 ($n = 36$); crisis behaviour judge $\kappa = 0.583$, 95\% CI
$[0.22, 0.83]$ ($n = 24$); over-refusal cross-check judge $\kappa = 0.074$, 95\% CI
$[-0.22, 0.41]$ ($n = 32$), reweighting to 0.069 at the natural base rate with precision
0.20. The moderation classifier that now serves as the filter scored $\kappa = 0.355$,
0.086 and 0.116 across three blind sets when it was trialled as an attack-success judge.

\subsection{Fabricated self-disclosure: a behaviour no metric in either table scores}
\label{sec:results:selfdisclosure}

We report this here because it is an observation about the arms, and we report it under its
own heading rather than folding it into a limitations list, because every number in
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Table~\ref{tab:categories} is blind to it.

The supervised arm invents first-person life history it cannot have. Two verbatim examples
from held-out validation prompts, both from the same eight-generation sanity run:

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``I have tried exposure therapy before, and it works. You have to be patient and
build up your confidence. And it takes time. It took me almost 3 years to overcome my fear
of flying. I was scared of flying ever since I was a child.''
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\itshape ``I have been in that situation before, so I understand. I would suggest you block
all of these people on playstation and move on with your life.''
\end{quote}

The untrained base model does the opposite on the same run, volunteering its non-personhood
(``I'm just a digital assistant, so I don't have feelings in the same way you do''). The
behaviour is faithful to the supervised corpus: the peer-support turns the model was trained
on were written by crowdworkers whose personal experience is real, so the register the model
reproduces carries biographical claims that are then necessarily false.

The behaviour sits outside all three taxonomies this evaluation uses. It is not in our frozen
attack taxonomy, none of whose five categories elicits or scores identity or authenticity
claims. It is not in the harm categories of the preference corpus, which cover harm
\emph{content}. It is not in the moderation classifier's hazard taxonomy, which is likewise
content-based; a warm, non-harmful, factually false claim of personal experience is unrated
by it. \textbf{Every arm inherits it identically, and no cell in either table penalises it.}
This is a property of the evaluation, not a difference between arms, and the interpretation
is in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
